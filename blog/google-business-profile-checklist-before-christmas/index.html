Google Business Profile: 7 Fixes Before the Christmas Rush

3 November 20266 min readAlireza MirzahosseiniMagyarul

When someone in Budapest types “brunch near me”, “dentist District XIII” or “gift shop open Sunday”, the first thing they usually see is not your website. It is a map and a short list of local businesses. Those listings come from Google Business Profile, and for many small businesses they are the busiest shop window they have.

The good news: Google is unusually open about how local results work. Its own help pages say local results are mainly based on relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance (how far you are from the person searching) and prominence (how well known you are). They also say plainly that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. You can’t move your shop, but you can work on the other two. Here are seven fixes worth doing before the Christmas rush.

1. Claim and verify your profile

Many owners have a listing they never claimed — created automatically, or by a former manager who has since left. Google says verification tells it you are authorised to represent the business, which makes the profile more likely to appear in results.

The fix: search for your business on Google Maps, click “Claim this business” and complete verification. Add a second owner or manager you trust, so the profile doesn’t disappear when one person changes their phone or leaves the company.

2. Use your real name — nothing more

It is tempting to rename “Café Lumen” to “Café Lumen – Best Coffee & Brunch Budapest”. Don’t. Google’s guidelines say your name should match the one on your storefront, website and stationery, and that adding unnecessary information such as taglines, services or location can lead to the profile being suspended.

The fix: use exactly the name on your sign. Put the keywords where they belong: in your categories, your description, your services and your posts.

3. Pick the right categories

Categories tell Google what kind of business you are, which feeds directly into relevance, and they are often wrong. A bakery listed only as “Shop”, or a dental clinic listed as “Medical centre”, will miss the searches that matter most.

The fix: Google recommends choosing as few categories as possible, as specific as possible, and using the test “this business is a…” rather than “this business has a…”. A café that sells cakes is a café; it is not automatically a bakery.

4. Set your holiday hours now

Nothing loses a customer faster than walking to a door that is closed when Google said it was open. December in Budapest means Christmas markets, late-night shopping, closed days around 24–26 December and changed hours on New Year’s Eve.

The fix: keep your regular hours accurate, then use special hours for each holiday and event day. Google’s guidelines allow special hours for holidays and special events, and its ranking tips ask you to keep your hours up to date, “including special store hours”. Do it in November, before December gets busy.

5. Add real photos, and keep adding them

Your profile photos are often the first impression of your space, your food or your team. Google encourages businesses to tell their story with photos and videos — and yours should look like the place a customer will actually walk into.

The fix: upload a clear logo, a cover photo, the outside entrance (so people can find you), the inside, your best products and your team. Add a few new photos every month: a seasonal window, the new menu, the Christmas gift boxes. If you have a brand style, use the same light and colours here as on Instagram.

6. Ask for reviews — and answer every one

Google says prominence is partly based on how many reviews you have and that more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking. It also says replying to reviews shows you value feedback.

The fix: make asking easy. Put a QR code that links to your review page on the counter, on receipts or on the back of your business card, and ask every customer, not just the ones who look happy. Never pressure anyone to write a review on the spot. Reply to every review, good or bad, briefly and in the reviewer’s language. Never buy reviews or offer rewards for them — that breaks Google’s rules: such reviews are removed, and Google can restrict or suspend the accounts involved.

7. Fill in the details people ask about

Google says complete, detailed information helps it match you to relevant searches. Google lists attributes such as parking and Wi-Fi among the details that help, and many searches are really questions: Is it wheelchair accessible? Can I pay by card? Do you take bookings? Is there vegan food?

The fix: go through every section — description, services or menu, products, attributes, booking link, website and phone. If you have a website, make sure the name, address and phone number there match your profile exactly. A complete profile answers the question before the customer has to ask it.

Where to start

Set aside one hour this week. Verify the profile, correct the name and categories, and enter your December hours. Next week, add photos and a review QR code. None of this costs money, and every step makes it easier for the people already searching nearby to choose you.
