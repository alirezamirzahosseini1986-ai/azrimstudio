5 Signs Your Brand Looks Cheap — and How to Fix Each One

27 October 20266 min readAlireza MirzahosseiniMagyarul

Most small businesses don’t look cheap because they spent too little. They look cheap because nobody made a few simple decisions — and then stuck to them. Customers rarely say “your typography is inconsistent”. They just trust you a little less, and walk into the café next door.

Here are the five signs we see most often when we look at cafés, shops, clinics and service businesses in Budapest, and what to do about each one this month.

1. Too many fonts

A menu with one font for the name, another for the headings, a third for the prices and a “fun” one for the specials tells guests that nobody is in charge. Every new typeface adds noise, not personality.

The fix: choose one type family and create contrast with weight and size instead. If you really need two, pair one display face for headlines with one clean text face for everything else — and write the rule down so the next flyer follows it too.

2. Colours with no system

The red on your sign, the red on your Instagram posts and the red on your menu are three different reds. Each one was “close enough” on the day. Together, they make the brand feel improvised.

The fix: define one main colour, one accent and one neutral, with exact codes (HEX for screens, CMYK or Pantone for print). Put them in a one-page document and share it with everyone who touches your materials — including the print shop.

3. A logo that vanishes when small

Most people first meet your logo as a tiny circle: an Instagram profile picture, a Google Maps pin, a favicon in a browser tab. Thin lines, long words and fine details disappear at that size, and the logo stops doing its job.

The fix: keep your full logo for signs and print, and add a simplified mark — an initial, a symbol or a short version of the name — for small sizes. Test it at the size of your profile picture before you approve it.

4. Stock photos everyone uses

The smiling barista, the handshake, the laptop on a white desk: customers have seen these on dozens of other websites. Free stock photos save money, but they also tell people you have nothing of your own to show.

The fix: photograph your real place, your real products and your real team. A modern phone, daylight and a clean background are enough to start. Ten honest photos beat a hundred generic ones.

5. Different everywhere

The sign says one thing, the website another and Instagram a third — different logos, different tones of voice, different promises. Brands are built by repetition. When nothing repeats, nothing is remembered.

The fix: create a simple style guide: logo versions, colours, fonts, photo style and three sentences about how you speak. Use it for every new piece, from the menu to the Google Business profile.

Where to start

You don’t need to rebrand everything at once. Pick the sign that hurts most, fix it, and apply the rule everywhere before moving to the next one. Small, consistent decisions are what make a business look established.
